Stage 10: does fine-tuning Kev on public, human-labelled record-matching pairs bring it to Jev's zero-shot level on matching data it was not trained on?

Why this stage. Stages 6 and 7 found that the open decision models cannot replace Jev as they ship, and that the gap is largest on record
matching. On the Walmart-Amazon test set Kev-4B called 104 non-matching pairs a match (Jev: 18). Recalibrating with 200 human labels closed
most of that gap in stage 6, but on new matching data in stage 7 two datasets stayed below Jev for each model after calibration (Kev-4B:
AUC 0.937 against 0.961 on Amazon-Google, recall 64% on DBLP-ACM), so part of the gap is ranking, which calibration cannot change. A
possible cause, not tested so far: no stage of Kev's training contains record pairs. Its base set decision-v7 is 10,000 records from ten
public classification datasets (Banking77, BoolQ, AG News, MultiNLI, SST-5, Yelp, TREC, DBpedia-14, Amazon reviews, IMDb) plus generated
policy and rule examples; the later stages add dates and missing evidence, consumer-finance complaints, generated skill data and developer
tooling (open_models/kev/README.md "Training"; docs/model-cards/kev-4b.md and kev-0.8b.md "Training data", read 2026-10-13). Kev ships its
training code and a documented way to continue from a released checkpoint, and its author reports +10 points of accuracy from 5,219
labelled in-domain records and nothing measurable from 400 (README, "Fine-Tune on Your Own Data"). Those gains are in-distribution. The
question here is the one Jev's zero-shot numbers pose: after seeing matching pairs from other sources, is Kev as good as Jev on a
matching dataset it has never seen?

Training data (database/p10_data.py)
- A training record is the request the evaluation sends - state {record_a, record_b}, the first-draft question of entity_match/run.py
  with its criteria, unchanged - plus the human label. Products and publications keep their own question.
- Per dataset: the first 2,000 pairs of its training split in the order of sha256("p10|<dataset>|<pair id>"). Matches and non-matches
  stay in their natural proportion; nothing is resampled, augmented or rewritten. The validation and test splits are never trained on.
- Arms, per model:
    loo-<D> (four arms, the test of this stage): trained on the other three datasets (6,000 pairs), evaluated on D.
    all (one arm, a reference): trained on all four (8,000 pairs), evaluated on all four.
    released: the v1.0 checkpoint as it ships, from stage 7's stored answers.

Training (database/p10_train.sh; one run per arm, no search)
- kev.train continued from the released checkpoint (--init_from), LoRA rank 16 and head as released, learning rate 2e-5, one epoch,
  effective batch 8 (1 x 8 accumulation), --p_none_pair 0.25, 2,000 replayed records of decision-v7's training part (--replay; the
  author's device against forgetting), seed 1, full-precision weights, PyTorch MPS. These are the settings of the last fine-tuning stage
  of the released checkpoints (their head.pt) and of the README's advice; none was tuned for this stage and none will be.
- One checkpoint per run, the final one. No validation-based selection, no early stopping, no second seed. A run that drops any record
  for length, or ends with a non-finite loss, does not count.

Temperature
- A fine-tuned checkpoint ships with temperature 1.0. Following the author's recipe, one temperature per checkpoint is fitted by maximum
  likelihood on human labels held out from training: the 200 validation pairs per dataset that stage 7 used (the first 200 of its label
  pool), taken from the arm's training datasets only (600 pairs for a loo arm, 800 for all). No label of the held-out dataset is used.
  p_T = sigmoid(logit(p_1) / T). T changes Brier and calibration, never accuracy at 0.5, AUC or F1. Raw (T = 1) numbers are reported too.

Evaluation
- Each checkpoint is served exactly as in stages 6-8 (kev.serve, MLX, bfloat16, sandboxed, concurrency 16) and asked the full test sets
  with the unchanged questions. A decision is p >= 0.5. No threshold is tuned.

Lines (per model)
- T, transfer - the line of this stage. For a held-out dataset D, the loo-D checkpoint with its fitted temperature is "on par with Jev"
  on D's full test set by the stage-6 rule: accuracy not more than 2.0 points below Jev's, AUC not more than 0.02 below, Brier not more
  than 0.02 above. Point estimates decide; a bootstrap over test pairs (2,000 resamples, paired with Jev's answers on the same pairs)
  gives 95% intervals, and an interval that contains a line is reported as not separable from it.
  "Fine-tuning brings the model to Jev's zero-shot level on matching" = on par on all four held-out datasets. Anything less is reported
  as the count out of four, naming the legs that failed.
- G, guard against forgetting, for every fine-tuned checkpoint: accuracy at most 2.0 points below the released checkpoint on each of
    g1  the first 1,000 review texts of stage 6 (question "positive?", human labels);
    g2  the first 100 restaurant sentences of stage 6 x 21 predicates, one question per request (mean over the 2,100 judgments);
    g3  the development split of decision-v7 (1,204 records, 1,468 questions; choice: the answered option, noul: p >= 0.5,
        score: the most probable level).
  A checkpoint that meets T and fails G is reported as "reaches Jev on matching but forgets", and does not count toward the verdict.
- The verdict for a model: T on all four datasets with G met by those four checkpoints.

Rules of collection
- One command per model. Order: fetch and check data, build the training files, then for each arm train, serve, ask, stop the server;
  then the analysis. A finished step is not repeated. Failed requests are re-asked in up to 4 passes; what still has no answer counts as
  a wrong decision and as p = 0.5, and is reported. A dataset with more than 5% of requests unanswered is judged "below".
- A training run that crashes is started again from the beginning once (adapter runs have no resume point); a second failure leaves
  that arm missing, and the model's verdict is then "incomplete".
- Until every arm of a model is collected, only counts, timings, memory and the trainer's own loss line are looked at; no answer is
  examined, and no question, data selection, setting, temperature rule or line is changed. Anything added afterwards is marked post hoc.
- No paid interface is called. Training and serving run sandboxed without network access; everything stays inside open_models/ and out/.

How the method above is carried out (fixed now, before any real pair or answer; where it narrows a sentence above, the reason is given)
- Temperature: "fitted by maximum likelihood, following the author's recipe" is the author's own fit (kev/metrics.py TEMPERATURE_FIT, used for the released checkpoints): the minimum mean negative log likelihood over a 121-point log grid from 0.25 to 4, on the served p clipped to [5e-5, 1 - 5e-5] (half the server's 4-decimal rounding step). The full rehearsal showed why the grid matters: a checkpoint that separates its pool labels perfectly has no finite maximum-likelihood temperature (an unbounded fit ran to T -> 0). A temperature at the end of the grid is flagged in the report.
- The released checkpoint's answers are replayed from the caches of phases 6-8, opened read-only; only what is not there is asked (counted on the real rows without asking anything: Kev-4B has every request except the 1,112 distinct development requests of g3; Kev-0.8B has only the test and pool requests of phase 7, so its g1, g2, g3, end-to-end and option-order requests are asked now). The name of a question is not part of what Kev reads (kev/api.py to_record keeps only instructions and options), so phase 7's requests through the SQL functions (one question named "q") are the same requests as this phase's (named "same"); the replay maps the names.
- g1 = the first 1,000 of phase 6's 5,000 review texts, scored per labelled review row as phase 6 scored them. g3 is scored per question (1,468); its 1,204 records contain 1,112 distinct requests, each asked once and scored once per record.
- A reading added, without a line: the released checkpoint with a temperature fitted the same way on the same pool labels as each loo arm, to show how much of a Brier change a temperature alone gives.
- Serving: each checkpoint on port 8015 with scripts/open_models.sh's environment, sandboxed, factory settings, concurrency 16. Nothing is asked if the server reports another run than the one requested (kev.serve silently falls back to runs/smoke when a run directory has no head.pt), if a fine-tuned checkpoint reports a temperature other than 1.0, or if its prefix cache is not at the factory size. Answers are cached under the arm's name plus a digest of its weights, so a retrained checkpoint never inherits an earlier one's answers.
- A checkpoint counts if its files are complete, its log has the wrapper's end line and the trainer's "saved" line, no record was dropped, truncated or rejected, and the trainer saw exactly the arm's records plus the 2,000 replayed (rule 3; a non-finite loss stops kev.train before it saves).
- Rules 2 and 7 in the script: a training run that fails or is stopped by the memory watchdog (footprint read every 5 s, 44 GB) is set aside, never deleted, and trained once more from the beginning; a second failure makes the arm missing. An unfinished run left by an interrupted invocation is set aside and trained again from the beginning. Rule 4: an evaluation step whose server does not start, answers as the wrong checkpoint or exits is done once more with a new server, the answers already given coming from the cache; a second failure makes the arm missing. The analysis runs once every arm is complete or missing for good.
