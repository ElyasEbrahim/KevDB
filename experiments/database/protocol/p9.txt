Stage 9: does the packed way of asking a semantic join (stage 8) carry over to a join that is not record matching?

Why this stage. Stage 8 found that, on Jev, sending a left record with its 10 candidates as 10 yes/no questions in one request (form Q,
the "star" layout of JEVDB) keeps F1, costs 38% of the input tokens and 10% of the time, and does not depend on the order of the
candidates. Both datasets were record matching, the one kind of predicate on which Jev was already known to be strong. This stage asks the
same three lines of a different predicate: whether two movie reviews express the same, or opposite, sentiment.

What was known when this was written
- Stage 1 (2026-10-07): Jev's pair-by-pair answers to both questions on all 8,128 pairs (cached; Q7, all opposite pairs: F1 0.732 by
  SemBench's evaluator, 153 s, 3.34M input tokens = 411 per pair), and the rewritten query that judges each of the 128 reviews once and
  joins on the two judgments (Q7r: F1 0.79, 128 requests). So for this particular predicate the best plan is already known and is not a
  packed pairwise form; what is tested here is only whether packing changes what the model answers about a pair, and what it costs.
- Stage 7 (2026-10-12): Kev-4B's pair-by-pair answers to "opposite" on all 8,128 pairs (cached; Q7 F1 0.530, 1,412 s, 144 tokens per pair).
- Stage 8 (2026-10-13), above. JEVDB reports quality deficits of its own on Movie Q5 and Q6, and no comparison between request layouts.
- Nothing about a packed form on any of these reviews: no such request has been sent to either model.
- Rehearsals on made-up reviews only (2026-10-13; 24 invented reviews, and one probe request per size with invented reviews). Jev accepts
  127 questions in one request: 0.78 s against 0.33 s for 40 (one request each), 10,341 input tokens. Input tokens were 40%, 33% and 28% of the
  pair form's at 5, 10 and 23 partners per request. Kev-4B at 10 partners: 77% of the tokens, 55% of the time. So before any real review was
  asked it was already likely that the cost line is met on Jev at every block size and missed on Kev-4B. Quality and order sensitivity on
  real reviews were not known; on the made-up reviews, which are trivially easy, every form gave the same decisions.

Data (SemBench movie scenario, the 2,000-row sample; database/p1_movie.py)
- The 128 distinct reviews of the movie used by Q5-Q7 (66 labelled positive, 62 negative) and all 8,128 pairs with reviewId1 < reviewId2.
  Truth, as in SemBench's gold SQL: "same" = equal human sentiment labels (4,036 pairs), "opposite" = different labels (4,092 pairs).
- Questions: the two stage-1 questions, unchanged ("same" for Q5, "opposite" for Q6 and Q7). No criteria, as in stage 1.

Forms (database/p9_join.py, build)
- pair  state = {review_1, review_2}, one yes/no question. Exactly the request of stage 1.
- Q     state = {review_1}; one yes/no question per partner, carrying the partner inside its instructions ({"question": ..., "review_2": ...}).
A review is paired with every review of larger id (up to 127 partners), cut into blocks of k partners: k = 10 (the block size of stage 8),
40, and 127 (one request per review). Order of the partners before cutting: random (by hash; primary), natural (by id), reversed. A different
order also puts different partners together in a block, so the order test covers the set effect too.
A decision on a pair: p >= 0.5. Nothing is tuned. Every step uses its own cache namespace, so every request of every step is asked fresh.

Backends
- jev: jev-1.13.0, official API, concurrency 16. Both questions; the pair form on all pairs, asked again today; Q at all three k in all
  three orders.
- kev-4b: as in stages 6-8 (kev-1.0, weights v1.0, MLX, shipped settings, sandboxed), concurrency 16. Only "opposite" (the one question
  its stage-7 answers cover completely), only k = 10, random and natural order. Its pair form is re-asked today on the pairs of 16 reviews
  (672 pairs) to time it; quality is measured against its stage-7 pair answers on all pairs (its answers are deterministic; the 672 re-asked
  pairs are compared with them as a control).

Measures
- Precision / recall / F1 over all 8,128 pairs against the human labels, per question.
- Reference: the pair form's answers on the same pairs (Jev: today's; control: agreement with the stage-1 answers, bar 97% as in stage 6).
- Cost: requests, input tokens as reported by the backend, wall time; per pair where the pair form was timed on a subset (Kev-4B).

Lines, per backend and block size k; a block size "holds" if all three are met on every question that backend is asked
- L1 quality: F1(Q, random order) >= F1(pair) - 1.0 point. The point estimate decides. The 95% interval of the difference comes from 2,000
  resamples of reviews, not of pairs (a review is in 127 pairs): a pair's weight is the product of the times its two reviews were drawn.
  An interval that contains -1.0 is reported as not separable from the line.
- L2 cost: input tokens <= 50% of the pair form's AND wall time <= 50% of the pair form's.
- L3 order: decisions agree on >= 99.0% of pairs between every two orders that were run, and their F1 values differ by <= 1.0 point.
"The stage-8 result carries over to this predicate" = k = 10 holds on Jev. The largest k that holds is reported as well.

Reported next to the lines, with no line of their own
- The F1 that SemBench's own evaluator gives Q7 for each form (a check on the F1 computed here).
- The rewritten plan of stage 1 on the same pairs (from its cached per-review judgments): how far any pairwise form is from it.
- How often the two questions, which are each other's negation, are answered alike for the same pair, pair form against Q.
- Agreement with the pair form, mean difference in probability, Brier score, calibration error, share of errors among the 30% least
  confident pairs; per-request latency (median and 95th percentile) at each k.

Rules of collection
- One command, database/p9_run.sh. A failed request is re-asked in up to 4 passes; what still has no answer counts as "not a match" and is
  reported. Jev spending is capped at $2.00 (expected: about $1.0); beyond it collection stops and what exists is reported.
- Until all collection is finished only counts, timings and token totals are looked at; no answer is examined and no question, order,
  block size or line is changed. Anything added after the results are seen is marked post hoc.
- Kev-4B is never fitted or calibrated on Jev's output (nothing in this stage fits anything).

A note on L3 that the post hoc figures below make necessary. Its 99.0% bar was taken from Jev's agreement with itself on earlier tasks
(99.45-99.8%). On these two questions Jev agrees with itself less: the same pair request asked three times on 2026-10-07 agrees
98.72-98.78%, today against 2026-10-07 98.6%, the same Q request asked twice today 98.14% ("same") and 98.73% ("opposite"). The agreement
between orders (98.13-98.68%) is at that level. So L3 is not met as written, but the bar was above what the same request achieves against
itself here, and the failure is not evidence of an order effect. The verdicts stand on L1.

  partner per request (k = 1) for "opposite" on all pairs.
