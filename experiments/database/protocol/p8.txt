Stage 8: how should a semantic join ask a decision model? One request per candidate pair, or one request per left record?

Why this stage. Every benefit that held in stages 1-7 was either independent of the model (join rewriting, packing predicates) or bound to
one situation (cascades on record matching, the text cache on long documents). The survey in misc/ResearchandInvestigation.md describes two
properties of decision models that the earlier stages never put to a test on a join: the state is encoded once and every question runs as
its own branch, and latency does not grow with the number of options. If they hold on real rows, the inner loop of a semantic nested-loop
join (one left record against its k candidates) fits in one request. The same survey lists the risk: answers depend on the order and the set
of options (its hard constraint 3, "permutation tests are required"), which no earlier stage measured. Prior work this is checked against:
selecting among candidates instead of matching pairs works for generative LLMs but has position bias (Wang et al., "Match, Compare, or
Select?", COLING 2025); a production engine rewrites semantic joins into multi-label classification (Cortex AISQL, arXiv 2511.07663);
JEVDB (arXiv 2610.02046) prunes candidate pairs first and then asks pair by pair.

What was known when this was written
- table_match/ (2026-10-05): Jev's pair-by-pair judgments on the same candidates. Abt-Buy, 10 candidates per left record (10,747 pairs):
  F1 90.7%, 543 input tokens per pair, 210 s. DBLP-Scholar (26,160 pairs): F1 90.7%, 461 input tokens per pair, 517 s.
- Nothing about any packed form on any real row: neither Jev nor Kev-4B has been sent one.
- A rehearsal on made-up rows only (2026-10-13; 12 invented left records x 12 invented candidates per dataset, in no dataset). Both
  backends accept all three packed forms. Jev: per-request latency did not grow with the number of candidates (0.31-0.35 s for 1, 5, 10 and
  12 candidates); input tokens for 10 candidates were 40% (Q), 39% (S) and 21% (C) of ten pair requests. Kev-4B: tokens 73% (Q, S) and 29% (C)
  of the pair requests, time 52-56% (Q, S) and 24% (C). So before any real row was asked it was already likely that the cost line below is met
  on Jev, and missed by Q and S on Kev-4B, where a request costs time in proportion to its tokens and carries little fixed overhead. Quality
  and order sensitivity on real rows were not known; on the made-up rows Jev's decisions were identical across forms and orders, Kev-4B's
  were not (S and C changed with the order). The lines are kept as written in database/p8_join.py before the rehearsal.

Data (table_match/, both with the complete human mapping; the stored BM25 candidates and the stage's first-draft questions are reused unchanged)
- abt-buy: 1,081 x 1,092 products, 1,097 true pairs, close to one-to-one. dblp-scholar: 2,616 x 64,263 publications, 5,347 true pairs, a
  left record often has several matches. k = 10 candidates per left record, as stored on 2026-10-02.
- Subset: the first 400 left records of each dataset by sha256("p8|<dataset>|<left id>"). Scaling rows: the first 80 of the abt-buy subset.

Forms (database/p8_join.py, build). The pair question and its criteria are the ones Jev answered in table_match/.
- pair  state = both records, one yes/no question. The baseline.
- Q     state = the left record; k yes/no questions, each carrying one candidate inside its instructions ({"question": ..., "record_b": ...}).
- S     state = the left record and all k candidates; k yes/no questions, each pointing at one candidate (`candidates.c3`).
- C     state = the left record; one choice question whose options are the k candidates plus "none". At most one match per left record,
        so it is a join method only where matches are close to one-to-one: run on abt-buy only.
Orders of the candidates inside a request: random (by hash; the primary order, neutral to position), rank (BM25 rank), reversed.
A decision on a candidate pair: p >= 0.5 for yes/no forms; "this candidate is the chosen option" for C. No threshold or prompt is tuned.

Backends
- jev: jev-1.13.0 through the official API, concurrency 16. Full tables in random order; the subset in all three orders; the pair form
  re-asked today on the subset (fresh cache namespace) for timing and as a drift control.
- kev-4b: exactly as in stages 6 and 7 (kev-1.0, weights v1.0, MLX, shipped settings, sandboxed), concurrency 16. Subset only, to keep the
  run near 100 minutes: pair; Q and S in random and rank order; C in all three orders.

Measures
- End-to-end precision / recall / F1 against the full mapping: true pairs that the candidate step missed count as misses, as in table_match/.
- Reference for quality: on Jev, the 2026-10-05 pair judgments on the full table (control: today's pair judgments on the subset must agree
  with them on at least 97% of pairs, the stage-6 bar; otherwise the comparison is flagged). On Kev-4B, its own pair judgments on the subset.
- Cost: requests; input tokens as reported by the backend; wall time of the subset runs, all asked fresh and one after another on the same day.

Lines (per backend; a packed form "holds" if all three are met on every dataset it is run on)
- L1 quality: F1(packed, random order) >= F1(pair) - 1.0 point. The point estimate decides; a paired bootstrap over left records
  (2,000 resamples) gives the 95% interval of the difference, and an interval that contains -1.0 is reported as not separable from the line.
- L2 cost: on the subset, input tokens <= 50% of the pair form's AND wall time <= 50% of the pair form's (requests are 1/k by construction).
- L3 order: on the subset, decisions agree on >= 99.0% of candidate pairs between every two orders that were run, and the F1 values of
  those orders differ by <= 1.0 point. (Jev asked twice agrees with itself on 99.45-99.8% of decisions.)

Reported next to the lines, with no line of their own
- Agreement of each packed form with the pair form and the mean difference in probability; Brier score, equal-mass calibration error and
  the share of errors among the 30% least confident pairs (is a cascade still possible on top of a packed form).
- C: accuracy by the position of the true match, how often the first three positions and "none" are chosen, accuracy above and below
  confidence 0.9.
- Scaling (Jev, 80 rows, concurrency 1, fresh): per-request latency and tokens for 1 (pair), 5, 10, 20 and 40 candidates, and whether the
  decisions on the five best-ranked candidates change as more candidates are added (the set effect).
- After the verdicts, a demonstration with no line: the packed form with the best full-table F1 among those that hold on Jev is added to
  src/jevsql as a SQL function, and the abt-buy join is run as one DuckDB statement over the stored candidates. Replayed from this stage's
  cache it must return exactly the matches of the script; one fresh run gives its end-to-end time and cost. Skipped if no form holds.

Rules of collection
- One command, database/p8_run.sh. A failed request is re-asked in up to 4 passes; what still has no answer counts as "not a match" and is
  reported. Jev spending is capped at $3.00 (expected: about $1.3); beyond it collection stops and what exists is reported.
- Until all collection is finished only counts, timings and token totals are looked at; no answer is examined and no question, order,
  threshold or line is changed. Anything added after the results are seen is marked post hoc.
- Kev-4B is never fitted or calibrated on Jev's output (nothing in this stage fits anything).

Correction to "Why this stage" above. It says JEVDB "prunes candidate pairs first and then asks pair by pair". That is wrong, and it was
written without checking the paper for it. Read again after the results (2026-10-13): JEVDB judges pairs with yes/no questions but sends
them in one of three request layouts - separate, packed ("bundles multiple independent string pairs into a single network payload") and
star ("attaches candidate counterparts as distinct questions within the same request", the anchor record sent once) - and picks between
star and pack by the average number of candidates per anchor. The star layout is this stage's form Q. So Q is not a new way of asking; what
this stage adds for it is the measurement the paper does not report: quality against the pair form on complete human mappings, tokens and
latency per request, and order tests. The paper does not use a choice question to pick a join partner and reports no order or permutation
test; form C and the order results are not covered by it. The lines and verdicts do not depend on this sentence.
