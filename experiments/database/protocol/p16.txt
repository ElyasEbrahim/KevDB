The owner's instruction (2026-10-31), after the plan protocol/p16_plan.md of 2026-10-30 and its three recommended decisions (scope as

QUESTIONS
1. Propagation: how do the cheap model's row-level errors turn into the error of a query's final answer, by query shape?
2. Allocation: if the engine buys human labels for rows chosen from the query (each row's influence on the answer), is the answer
   reliable against human labels at lower cost than with designs that ignore the query or rely on the model's confidence?
3. Systematic bias and correlated errors: does the cheap model's error depend on the group; does a second model share its errors, so that
   using it as the reference gives a confidently wrong answer; does a human pilot (instead of confidence) change cost or validity?
Prior work (checked in the papers' text, p16_plan.md section 2) already allocates by GROUP BY with guarantees relative to an oracle
(ABae-GroupBy, BaS) and by influence in statistics (active inference, OPAL). This phase proposes no new estimator; it tests the
engine-level combination (influences from the plan, a design-based correction, residuals measured by a human pilot) against them.

DESIGNS (each gives the inclusion probability pi_i of the final labelled set; labels of a row cost once however often it is drawn)
Water-filling W(u, T): pi_i = min(1, lambda u_i) with lambda such that the sum is T. Mixture: pi <- .8 pi + .2 T/n (all designs except
U, labels-only, global calibration and abae). Single-stage designs are computed per fold j with target B N_j/N.
U    pi = B/N.
C    u_i = sqrt(p_i(1-p_i)) on D1; sqrt(sum_c p_ic(1-p_ic)) on D2 (raw cheap probabilities).
QU   u_i = sqrt(sum_k a_ik^2/h_k^2).
QC   u_i = sqrt(sum_k a_ik^2 p_ic(1-p_ic)/h_k^2), c the predicate of output k; on D2 h_k is replaced by max(.01, .25 mean_i p_ik).
Two-stage designs (QP, G, D): stage 1 pi1 = .3 B/N for every row. For the rows of fold j, everything in stage 2 is computed from the
other fold's stage-1 sample only (S1 of fold 1-j): a linear calibration c(p) = alpha + beta p fitted by least squares on it (per
predicate); squared residuals r^2 = (y - c(p))^2 of its rows; per cell the shrunk mean squared residual
  s2_cell = (n_cell m_cell + 10 m_bin)/(n_cell + 10), m = mean r^2 over the stage-1 rows of the cell, the cell's score bin (or all rows
  if the bin has none);
scores u_i below; target T2_j = (B N_j/N - N_j pi1)/(1 - pi1); pi2 = mixture(W(u, T2_j)) within fold j; final pi_i = 1 - (1-pi1)(1-pi2_i).
QP   (primary) u_i = sqrt(sum_k a_ik^2 s2_cell(i,c)/h_k^2); on D2 h_k is replaced by max(.01, .25 hbar_k), hbar_k = mean_i c_k(p_ik).

D    (Q5) as QP with h_k = max(.05, |t_k - .60|), t_k = sum_i a_ik c(p_i) (the calibrated cheap share of movie k).

stage-1 labels per group; stage 1 = .3 B labels split equally over strata (capped by stratum size, the remainder split again), simple
random samples within strata; a stratum's stage-1 standard deviation of a 0/1 statistic is sqrt(m(1-m)) with m = (sum y + 1)/(n + 2),
so that a stratum whose few stage-1 labels agree is not taken as certain; stage 2 = .7 B: scalar queries Neyman over strata on the
statistic (y; k y for Q4b), group queries
minimax over groups (Neyman within a group, group budgets equalising the estimated variance of the group estimates); estimator: the
stratified mean pooling both stages, variance sum W_s^2 (1 - n_s/N_s) var_s/n_s (var_s = ybar_s(1-ybar_s) if n_s = 1); Q3a, Q3b, Q4a are
combined from the per-movie estimates with their weights. Q6: one ABae per predicate with B/21 sentences each, each predicate estimated
from its own sample only (the per-operator approach).
labels-only  U's sample, Hajek estimate per output (labelled rows only); an output without labelled rows gets estimate .5 and the

global calibration  U's sample; c(p) fitted on all labelled rows; theta_k = sum_i a_ik c(p_i); variance from the coefficients' sandwich

COMMON ESTIMATOR (U, C, QU, QC, QP, G, D)
Cross-fitted calibration: rows of fold j use c fitted on fold 1-j (single-stage: all its labelled rows, weights 1/pi; two-stage: its
stage-1 rows); fewer than 10 rows or no spread in p -> c(p) = p. With yhat = c(p):
  theta_k = sum_i a_ik yhat_i + sum_i xi_i a_ik (y_i - yhat_i)/pi_i,   V_k = sum_i xi_i a_ik^2 (y_i - yhat_i)^2 (1 - pi_i)/pi_i^2.
Because c and pi of a row depend only on the other fold's sampling, the estimate is exactly unbiased under every design.
INTERVALS (every design): theta_k +- t sqrt(V_k), t the .975 quantile of Student's t with df_k degrees of freedom, clipped to [0, 1];
df_k = labelled rows of output k's support drawn with pi < 1, minus 1 (labels-only, abae and S5: labelled rows of the support minus 1;
global calibration: labelled rows minus 2). V_k = 0 (every row of the support drawn with certainty) gives the point; V_k > 0 with
df_k < 1 gives [0, 1]. Q5's family-wise intervals use the same rule at level 1 - .025/K.

2026-10-31T03:55:51Z  Deviation 1, found after the first formal run (03:50:11-03:53:24Z), before any result was reported. A bug against the standard's interval rule.
The rule above gives a zero-width interval when "V_k = 0 (every row of the support drawn with certainty)". The code gave it whenever
V_k = 0, which also happens when no row of the support was labelled where the inclusion probability is below 1 (an output with no labels
under U, C, QU, QC; a labels-only group whose single label or equal labels give no spread). Such an output then reported the cheap
model's estimate as a point, which is not what the rule says. Seen in the first run's Q5 table: U and C "decided" movies wrongly in 98%
of replications through these points. Fix (p16_methods.interval, p16_run.Accumulator): a zero-width interval only when every row of the
output's support is labelled; any other V_k = 0 gives [0, 1]; a test checks it. Every cell is run again into out/database/p16; the first
