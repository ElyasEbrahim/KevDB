Phase 11: why did fine-tuning not improve Kev-4B on dblp-acm in phase 10?

Why this phase. In phase 10 (protocol/p10.txt), Kev-4B fine-tuned on 6,000 human-labelled pairs from the three product datasets reached
Jev's zero-shot level on two unseen product datasets, but on dblp-acm, a publication dataset asked with a question that never appears in
its training data, its F1 did not move: 0.742 released, 0.742 fine-tuned (difference -0.001, -0.013 to +0.011); Jev 0.973. The direction of
the errors suggests why. The released Kev-4B is too strict on dblp-acm (39 false matches, 159 missed matches of 444), and product training
mainly removes false matches (walmart-amazon 104 -> 16), so it made Kev-4B, if anything, stricter (26 false, 167 missed). Kev-0.8B, too
lenient on dblp-acm when released (353 false, 0 missed), improved there from 0.716 to 0.940. Three explanations remain open: (a) the model
learned the product question's wording rather than the task; (b) it needs to have seen other kinds of records; (c) it needs to have seen
the publication question, or records like dblp-acm's, at all. Each arm below adds one ingredient to the phase 10 control.

Reading the arms (written now)
- pubs meets P2 and neither words nor domains does: in phase 10 the publication question or its kind of records was missing from training;
  transfer works within a question across datasets, not across questions, on this evidence.
- words meets P2: varied wording of one question helps a new question; reword questions in fine-tuning data.
- domains meets P2: varied kinds of records help a new question.
- no arm meets P2: one more similar dataset is not enough either; reported as such.
- Readings without a line: false and missed matches against the control (does the arm fix the strictness?), the temperature, the
  differences against Jev with intervals, and the end-to-end F1 on dblp-scholar for words and domains.
